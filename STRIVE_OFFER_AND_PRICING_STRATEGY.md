# Strive Offer and Pricing Strategy

**Prepared:** September 1, 2026

**Status:** Working commercial strategy; owner and qualified legal review required before production publication or use in an agreement
**Scope:** Offer architecture, pricing position, cancellation direction, ownership, support, and offboarding. This is not legal, tax, accounting, or financial advice.

**Authority:** This is the canonical commercial-strategy source. Other repository documents should link here instead of restating offer, pricing, cancellation, ownership, or offboarding language that could drift. Initial-launch approvals are recorded in `OWNER_LAUNCH_DECISIONS.md`.

## Executive decision

Strive should sell three clearly separated forms of work:

1. **Paid initial implementation** for websites, reporting systems, analytics implementation, and workflow automation.
2. **Optional ongoing service** for defined maintenance, reporting support, monitored automation, content changes, technical support, or continuous improvement.
3. **Separately approved expansion work** when a request materially changes the agreed scope or exceeds an ongoing-service boundary.

The recommended pricing presentation is a **hybrid model**:

- Use custom written proposals now because scope, delivery history, minimum viable engagement size, and margins are not yet approved.
- Explain the cost drivers publicly without publishing invented figures.
- After Strive has repeatable delivery data, publish starting prices or ranges only for services with stable inputs, boundaries, and unit economics.
- Keep complex integrations and cross-system work custom-scoped even if simpler offers later receive public ranges.

This protects the premium position, gives buyers commercial clarity, and avoids turning an unvalidated number into an implied promise.

## Positioning

### Standardized working direction; owner approval required

Strive is a lean, technically capable partner for growing small and midsize service businesses that need a clearer website, more useful reporting, or less repetitive operating work.

The credible advantage is the operating model:

- delivery stays close to the people responsible for the work;
- the business problem, deliverables, exclusions, responsibilities, and acceptance checks are written down;
- initial projects and optional ongoing services use different commercial terms;
- ownership, licensed materials, account access, documentation, and handoff are addressed before work begins;
- eligible ongoing monthly services can operate without a long-term commitment;
- material additions require a new scope or change order.

### Claims not approved

Do not say Strive is cheaper, more talented, the best, risk-free, guaranteed to produce results, or universally faster than another provider. Do not say every engagement is cancellable at any time or without cost.

“Less traditional agency overhead” and “direct access to the people performing the work” are publishable only after the owner documents the actual staffing, communication path, and account-management model. Until then, use “lean delivery,” “direct accountability,” and “communication paths defined in scope.”

## Ideal client

The primary client is a growing small or midsize service business that:

- has an outdated or unclear customer-facing website;
- relies on disconnected tools or reporting views;
- has repeat administrative work that can be mapped and improved;
- can identify a decision-maker and provide timely access, content, data context, and review;
- values a written scope and reviewable delivery over an undefined list of tasks;
- wants an initial implementation with optional continuity rather than an automatically bundled long-term retainer.

Fit must still be assessed individually. Strive should not imply suitability for regulated, high-risk, round-the-clock, or specialist work until its capabilities, insurance, security practices, staffing, and contract terms support those engagements.

## Commercial architecture

| Model           | Appropriate work                                                                                                            | Commercial basis                                                                                                                         | Ending the work                                                                                                                             |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Initial project | Website build or redesign, analytics implementation, reporting system, workflow automation, audit or discovery              | Approved fixed scope or paid discovery; owner-approved setup fee, deposit, or milestone schedule                                         | Written agreement governs payment for completed and in-progress work, nonrecoverable committed costs, transition, and any refund treatment  |
| Ongoing service | Defined maintenance, reporting support, monitored automation, content changes, technical support, or continuous improvement | Optional recurring scope with explicit inclusions, exclusions, renewal date, billing period, support boundaries, and cancellation method | Eligible monthly service may be canceled before the next renewal; treatment of the active period and offboarding is stated in the agreement |
| Expansion       | New feature, integration, workflow, major content volume, platform migration, or work outside the recurring boundary        | Separately approved scope, project, or change order                                                                                      | Follows the approved terms for that additional work                                                                                         |

An ongoing service must not be used to hide the recovery cost of a custom implementation. Upfront labor needs to be covered by the initial project economics even if the client ends optional monthly service at the first eligible cancellation point.

## Initial projects

### Typical deliverables

The exact list varies, but a project scope may include:

- discovery record and current-state findings;
- information architecture, content structure, or conversion-path definition;
- approved design and implementation outputs;
- measurement plan, event definitions, dashboard, or data-quality checks;
- workflow map, integration logic, exception paths, and human-review controls;
- validation record against agreed acceptance criteria;
- account/access inventory, documentation, and handoff record.

### Typical exclusions

Unless explicitly included, a scope should not imply:

- unlimited revisions or unlimited content entry;
- ongoing maintenance, monitoring, support, or continuous improvement;
- third-party subscription fees, licenses, media, or specialist services;
- legal, accounting, compliance, security certification, or accessibility certification;
- guaranteed rankings, leads, revenue, time savings, availability, or performance results;
- work on systems or integrations not identified during scope approval;
- emergency or out-of-hours support.

### Client responsibilities

The agreement should identify responsibility for:

- a decision-maker and review participants;
- accurate content, data context, and business rules;
- timely access to accounts, platforms, and approved credentials channel;
- third-party approvals and fees;
- feedback and acceptance decisions within an agreed process;
- legal, privacy, brand, and regulatory approvals belonging to the client;
- notification when assumptions, systems, or priorities change.

### Setup, deposit, and billing direction

Choose one owner-approved structure by project type:

- paid discovery before a final implementation scope;
- a deposit followed by milestone payments;
- a setup or implementation fee followed by the remaining approved project schedule.

Do not advertise a universal percentage, amount, installment schedule, or refund rule until cash flow, tax treatment, payment costs, and delivery economics have been modeled. The proposal should state when work starts, what triggers each invoice, whether amounts are refundable, and how client delay affects the schedule.

### Project cancellation direction

A project is not a freely cancellable monthly subscription. The agreement should define:

- notice method and effective date;
- payment for completed work and work already in progress;
- nonrecoverable third-party or specialist costs already committed with approval;
- treatment of deposits, milestones, expenses, and any refund calculation;
- the condition and format of deliverables transferred after required payment;
- access return, credential rotation, data handling, and separately scoped transition assistance.

The owner and counsel must approve the final formula. Public copy should point to the approved project agreement rather than improvise a universal refund promise.

## Ongoing services

### Eligibility

Only an offering with repeatable boundaries, recoverable setup costs, known delivery capacity, and an operational cancellation path should be described as month to month. The signed scope should identify the systems covered, included work, excluded work, review cadence, support channel, third-party dependencies, and any usage or volume limits.

### Working cancellation policy

> **Owner and qualified legal review required before production publication:** Ongoing monthly services do not require a long-term commitment. You may cancel before your next renewal, and future recurring charges will stop according to your agreement. Initial projects, completed work, committed third-party costs, active billing periods, and handoff responsibilities are governed by the approved project agreement.

The final recurring agreement must also define:

- the renewal date and billing frequency;
- the cancellation channel and information needed to identify the account;
- the cutoff for the next renewal and when cancellation becomes effective;
- whether the active period runs to its end, is prorated, or is otherwise treated;
- whether already-earned or already-committed charges are refundable;
- what service remains available during the active period;
- final invoice, access, export, documentation, and transition duties;
- retention or deletion of project and inquiry data after the relationship ends.

Avoid the phrase “cancel anytime” unless the exact mechanics make it literally true. Avoid “no contract” because a written agreement is still needed; “no long-term commitment for eligible monthly services” is more accurate.

## Change control

Every scope needs a simple change path:

1. Record the requested change and the business reason.
2. Identify its effect on deliverables, dependencies, cost, review effort, and schedule.
3. Decide whether it is included, replaces other work, or requires a change order or separate project.
4. Obtain written approval before additional work begins.
5. Update the decision record and acceptance criteria.

An ongoing plan should have explicit boundaries so a recurring fee does not become an unlimited queue. Unused capacity, rollover, priority, emergency handling, and overage treatment all require owner decisions before being offered.

## Ownership and licensing

The agreement—not a generic website sentence—must distinguish:

- client-provided content, data, trademarks, domains, and existing systems;
- client-owned accounts where practical;
- custom deliverables and when ownership or a license transfers after payment;
- Strive’s pre-existing materials, reusable systems, templates, methods, libraries, and know-how;
- open-source and third-party assets, software, subscriptions, and license restrictions;
- credentials, source files, design files, documentation, export formats, and portfolio rights.

The intended client experience is ownership clarity and a documented handoff. The precise transfer language remains a counsel-reviewed contract decision.

## Support boundaries

Ongoing service should define, at minimum:

- covered systems and excluded systems;
- included maintenance, monitoring, reporting, content, or improvement work;
- communication and request channels;
- business hours and time zone, if any are promised;
- priority definitions and response targets only when operationally supported;
- maintenance windows, backups, restoration, incidents, and third-party outages;
- client responsibilities, approval needs, and access requirements;
- what becomes separately scoped work.

Do not publish response times, availability, support levels, or service-level guarantees until staffing and monitoring evidence supports them.

## Offboarding

The applicable scope should make offboarding predictable. Depending on the engagement, it may include:

- confirming the effective end date and final covered period;
- reconciling approved invoices and committed third-party costs;
- transferring or confirming client-controlled accounts;
- returning, rotating, or revoking access and credentials;
- delivering the agreed files, exports, documentation, and status record;
- identifying third-party subscriptions the client must continue, replace, or cancel;
- describing data retention and deletion responsibilities;
- separately scoping any migration or transition assistance beyond the included handoff.

Straightforward offboarding means the process is defined in advance. It does not mean unlimited, immediate, or free transition work.

## Pricing presentation recommendation

### Use now: custom proposals with transparent cost drivers

Public copy should explain that price follows:

- the business problem and required discovery;
- agreed deliverables and acceptance criteria;
- content, platform, data, and integration complexity;
- client and third-party dependencies;
- migration, testing, documentation, and handoff requirements;
- the level and boundary of optional ongoing involvement.

This is more useful than a placeholder “contact for pricing” and more honest than an unvalidated starting price.

### Add later: selective starting prices or ranges

Publish a range only after the owner can show that:

- the offering has a repeatable scope and exclusion list;
- actual labor and vendor costs have been recorded;
- the minimum price covers sales, discovery, delivery, quality assurance, rework, project management, handoff, overhead, payment costs, and a target profit;
- client-delay and change-order rules protect capacity;
- the number can be honored for a clearly stated baseline configuration.

Use ranges for genuinely repeatable offers and custom proposals for complex work. Do not use low prices as the main brand signal.

## Owner decisions required

### Offer and fit

- Which exact services are initial projects, eligible monthly services, or unavailable?
- Is discovery separately paid, credited, or included by project type?
- What minimum engagement size and client-fit rules can be used internally?
- Who is accountable for sales, scope, delivery, support, billing, and offboarding?

### Project commercial terms

- Deposit, setup, milestone, final-payment, expense, tax, and late-payment rules
- Invoice triggers, payment methods, acceptance criteria, review windows, and client-delay treatment
- Cancellation notice, earned-work calculation, work-in-progress treatment, committed-cost treatment, refund formula, and transition scope
- Change-order approval, minimum change size, schedule effect, and authority to approve

### Recurring commercial terms

- Which offerings are eligible for month-to-month service?
- Billing in advance or arrears, renewal date, and cancellation cutoff
- Cancellation method, effective date, confirmation, and authorized requester
- Active-period service and refund/proration treatment
- Included capacity, volume, rollover, overage, queue priority, and emergency work
- Suspension, failed payment, third-party outage, and restart treatment

### Ownership and offboarding

- Client-owned account policy and exceptions
- Transfer timing for custom deliverables and licenses for embedded Strive materials
- Source, design, data, credential, documentation, domain, and third-party subscription treatment
- Included offboarding work, additional transition rate or scope, export format, and post-termination retention/deletion

### Pricing and economics

- Target gross margin or contribution requirement by service
- Minimum implementation price and minimum recurring fee
- Sales, discovery, setup, quality assurance, support, and offboarding labor assumptions
- Contractor, software, hosting, payment-processing, tax, insurance, and bad-debt costs
- Capacity by role, utilization target, support interruption cost, and churn tolerance
- Whether the current project-brief investment bands match the clients Strive can serve profitably

## Unit-economics risks

- **Unrecovered setup:** low or waived implementation fees can make first-renewal cancellation unprofitable.
- **Unlimited recurring scope:** vague “support” can consume more labor than the monthly fee covers.
- **Hidden handoff cost:** exports, migration, documentation, and credential cleanup require reserved capacity.
- **Third-party exposure:** nonrefundable vendors or annual subscriptions can outlast the client relationship.
- **Client-delay capacity loss:** blocked work can occupy the schedule without producing a billable milestone.
- **Change leakage:** small additions accumulate when the change-order threshold is undefined.
- **Support interruption:** reactive requests reduce planned delivery capacity unless priority and availability are priced.
- **Payment and tax friction:** processing fees, taxes, late payments, disputes, and refunds affect collected margin.
- **Acquisition cost:** lead review, discovery, proposals, and follow-up must be recovered across won work.
- **Concentration and churn:** a lean provider needs a capacity buffer and must not make monthly flexibility depend on replacing revenue immediately.

## Legal review items

Qualified counsel should review the applicable jurisdictions and agreement stack, including:

- entity identity, contract formation, proposal/SOW precedence, electronic acceptance, governing law, and dispute process;
- recurring authorization, material disclosures, renewal notices, cancellation channel, effective date, confirmation, and recordkeeping;
- state automatic-renewal laws and whether business-to-business or consumer rules apply to each offer and client;
- deposits, earned fees, work in progress, committed costs, refunds, taxes, late payments, suspension, termination, and collections;
- intellectual property transfer, background IP, open-source and third-party licensing, account ownership, and portfolio rights;
- confidentiality, data handling, security responsibilities, retention, deletion, export, and incident terms;
- warranties, disclaimers, liability, indemnity, insurance, force majeure, and service-level language.

Current-law note: the FTC’s 2024 amended Negative Option Rule was vacated by the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit in July 2025, and the FTC formally withdrew those amendments in February 2026. The older rule remains narrower, and federal/state statutes and enforcement against deceptive recurring practices can still apply. Strive should not present the withdrawn “click-to-cancel” rule as controlling law. Clear material terms, documented consent, an understandable cancellation path, and jurisdiction-specific counsel review remain prudent controls. See the [FTC’s current rule page](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/negative-option-rule), the [Eighth Circuit decision](https://ecf.ca8.uscourts.gov/opndir/25/07/243137P.pdf), the [FTC withdrawal notice](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/federal-register-notices/revision-negative-option-rule-withdrawal-cars-rule-removal-non-compete-rule-conform-these-rules), and a [2026 FTC subscription enforcement example](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/05/shutterstock-pay-35-million-settle-ftc-allegations-over-illegal-subscription-and-cancellation-practices).

## Implementation acceptance criteria

- Initial projects, eligible ongoing services, and expanded work are visibly distinct.
- Public copy never implies that a custom implementation can be abandoned without paying for earned work or committed cost.
- Eligible monthly flexibility is qualified and tied to the written agreement.
- No actual price, margin, response time, support level, refund rule, or timeline is invented.
- Ownership language identifies categories but leaves precise transfer mechanics to the agreement.
- FAQ structured data matches the visible FAQ copy.
- Inquiry retention and client-project retention remain separate concepts.
- The owner-decision and legal-review registers contain every unresolved commercial fact.
- Build, lint, type checks, tests, accessibility, links, reduced motion, and responsive overflow checks pass after the copy update.
- Nothing is pushed, merged, or deployed.
